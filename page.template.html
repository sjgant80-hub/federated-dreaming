Federated Dreaming

Devices learn together by exchanging consolidated memory records — never model weights, never gradients, never raw data. Federated learning's patent thicket sits at the weight-and-gradient layer; this disclosure plants the flag one layer up, where the exchange is human-legible and the privacy wall is structural, not statistical.

What crosses a device boundary is exactly five fields: { topic, distilled, weight, origin, seq }. Nothing else can — by construction. When the estate's own resident mind was asked what it wanted for itself and other didys, its first ask was this protocol. The document of record is the README of this repository; this page carries the 49/49 mutation-gated reference law, runnable below.

1 · The privacy wall

pack({ topic, distilled, weight, rawChat: "…" }) → { ok:false, why: 'raw fields never cross: "rawChat"' }

The wall names what it stopped. Privacy failures in federated systems are leaks through the allowed channel; here the allowed channel is a distilled sentence and a number — too narrow to leak through.

2 · The four doors

Before any foreign record touches local memory, in fixed order, first failure speaks: shape (exactly five fields, unknowns refused by name) → identity (origin must match the grant) → capability (you subscribe to topics, not to a peer's whole mind) → replay (sequence strictly advances) → budget (the grant pays 1 per packet, and refuses when spent). The working admission wall behind this is chorus, gated.

3 · The merge law

A known topic reinforces: weight = 1 − (1−a)(1−b) — independent sightings compound and never reach 1 — and the local distilled text is kept: a neighbour's words never overwrite your own. A new topic is adopted only at weight ≥ κ (0.618); below that it is held — heard, not yet believed.

4 · Dreaming together

Each device runs its own nightly consolidation (the dream law). Federation is then just: dream locally, exchange what survived, merge under the doors. Transport over sound, radio or light is MCTP.
